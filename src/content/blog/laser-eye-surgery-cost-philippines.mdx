## How CLEAR Eye Surgery Works

CLEAR (Corneal Lenticule Extraction for Advanced Refractive) is an advanced laser-assisted technique designed to treat common vision problems such as myopia (nearsightedness), hyperopia (farsightedness), and astigmatism. Unlike traditional LASIK procedures, which require creating a flap on the corneal tissue, CLEAR works through a minimally invasive approach. A femtosecond laser creates a tiny lenticule (a lens-shaped piece of corneal tissue) inside the cornea, which is then removed through a small incision.

This bladeless LASIK alternative maintains more of the eye’s structural integrity and reduces the risks of flap-related complications. Because CLEAR is minimally invasive, it is especially suitable for patients with thin corneas who may not qualify for conventional situ keratomileusis procedures.

The technique also reduces nerve disruption, meaning fewer patients experience dry eyes after surgery. By using advanced technology, CLEAR gives patients a chance to achieve clearer vision without depending on reading glasses or contact lenses.

## Benefits of CLEAR Compared to SMILE and LASIK

Many patients are familiar with LASIK surgery and SMILE, but fewer understand the differences with CLEAR. LASIK has been performed for decades and remains effective, yet it involves cutting a flap on the corneal tissue. While safe, this flap can increase risks in certain patients, especially those involved in contact sports or with naturally thin corneas.

SMILE surgery also uses a femtosecond laser but requires slightly larger incisions compared to CLEAR. This may increase healing time and discomfort in some cases. CLEAR improves on this by combining the precision of LASIK with the minimally invasive nature of SMILE, creating the perfect balance for many patients.

Patients who undergo CLEAR at Focus Vision frequently report less discomfort, quicker healing, and more stable results compared to older methods. For those struggling with presbyopia or near vision issues, CLEAR can also be combined with laser-blended vision to reduce dependence on reading glasses.

**In short,** CLEAR represents the next evolution in laser eye treatment, offering maximum benefits with fewer risks.

## Comparing CLEAR with LASIK Eye Surgery and Cataract Surgery

When patients consider vision correction, it’s important to discuss how CLEAR compares not only to LASIK eye surgery but also to other procedures like cataract surgery. While CLEAR focuses on reshaping the corneal tissue to correct refractive errors, cataract surgery involves replacing the natural lens that has become clouded, allowing more light to pass through and restoring clear vision. LASIK, on the other hand, uses a flap-based method to reshape the cornea, which can be highly effective but may not be suitable for patients with thin corneas or those seeking flap-free options.

The choice between these procedures depends on the following factors: your age, the condition of your eyes, whether you have cataracts, and whether your goal is to correct vision problems in both eyes or just one eye. Because there are several factors to weigh, including safety, recovery time, and long-term outcomes, patients should consult with an ophthalmologist to carefully evaluate their options.
